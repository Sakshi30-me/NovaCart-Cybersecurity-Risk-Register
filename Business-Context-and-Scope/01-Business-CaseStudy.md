### 1. Organisation Overview

NovaCart Retail Ltd. is a fictional, medium-sized e-commerce organisation headquartered in London, United Kingdom, with business operations serving customers across multiple international markets. Established in 2018, the organisation specialises in the online sale of consumer electronics, home appliances, and lifestyle products through its customer-facing e-commerce website and mobile application.

The organisation employs approximately 650 personnel and generates an estimated annual revenue of USD 45 million. Its business operations depend significantly on digital platforms, cloud-based infrastructure, electronic payment services, and third-party business relationships. The organisation's continued growth and international presence have increased its reliance on interconnected technology systems and the processing of customer and business information across its operating environment.

As a digitally dependent organisation, NovaCart must maintain the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of its information assets and supporting technology infrastructure to ensure business continuity, protect customer information, and maintain stakeholder confidence.

### 2. Business Operations and Technology Environment

NovaCart conducts its primary commercial activities through its e-commerce website and mobile application, which allow customers to browse products, create accounts, place orders, complete payments, and track deliveries. The organisation uses cloud-based infrastructure hosted on Amazon Web Services (AWS) to support its digital services, application workloads, and associated data repositories.

In addition to its customer-facing platforms, NovaCart uses enterprise business applications to manage customer relationships, financial operations, procurement, inventory, and internal administrative activities. These systems support different business functions and enable employees to access information required for their operational responsibilities.

The organisation integrates its digital platforms with external payment service providers to facilitate customer transactions. Third-party logistics providers support product storage, order fulfilment, and international delivery operations, while external marketing and customer engagement services support promotional activities and customer communications.

Employees access organisational systems through corporate user accounts and company-managed devices. Certain personnel require remote access to business applications to support operational activities across different geographical locations. These arrangements introduce dependencies on identity management, endpoint security, network protection, and access control mechanisms.

The interconnected nature of NovaCart's technology environment means that a security incident affecting one system, user account, or external service provider could potentially affect other business applications and operational processes.

### 3. Information Assets and Data Processing

NovaCart collects, processes, stores, and exchanges information required to support its commercial activities and internal business operations. Customer information includes names, email addresses, telephone numbers, delivery addresses, account information, and purchase histories. This information is used to manage customer accounts, process orders, provide customer support, and support business analytics.

Payment transactions are facilitated through external payment service providers. The organisation retains relevant transaction references and tokenized payment information for business and reconciliation purposes. The precise scope of payment information retained by NovaCart and the responsibilities of its payment providers will require further confirmation during the assessment.

Other information maintained by the organisation includes employee records, supplier information, financial records, procurement information, inventory data, contractual documentation, and internal operational records.

These information assets support critical business activities and may contain personal, confidential, or commercially sensitive information. Their unauthorised disclosure, alteration, destruction, or unavailability could affect customer trust, operational efficiency, financial performance, and the organisation's ability to meet applicable contractual and regulatory obligations.

### 4. Existing Information Security Environment

NovaCart reports having implemented foundational information security measures to protect its business applications, information assets, and technology infrastructure. These measures include role-based access control, firewall protection, endpoint antivirus software, periodic vulnerability scanning, daily database backups, and annual employee security awareness training.

The organisation also maintains basic supplier security review procedures and an incident response process intended to support the identification, escalation, and management of cybersecurity incidents.

However, the existence of these measures does not independently establish their effectiveness or consistent implementation. Further information is required to determine the extent of multi-factor authentication coverage, privileged access management, security monitoring, vulnerability remediation, backup restoration testing, and third-party security assurance.

The effectiveness of the existing security environment will therefore need to be evaluated using relevant documentation, stakeholder information, and supporting evidence during the cybersecurity risk assessment.

### 5. Historical Cybersecurity Incidents

NovaCart has experienced several security-related incidents during its previous business operations, highlighting potential weaknesses and areas requiring further investigation.

In 2024, a phishing incident resulted in the compromise of two employee accounts. This incident demonstrates the potential exposure of organisational identities to social engineering and credential-related threats.

In 2025, an e-commerce service disruption caused the customer-facing website to become unavailable for approximately three hours. Given the organisation's dependence on its online sales channels, service interruptions may affect customer experience, revenue generation, and business operations.

In 2026, a third-party supplier experienced a data exposure incident involving certain customer contact information. This incident highlights the potential risks associated with external service providers that process or access organisational information.

Although these incidents were reportedly addressed, detailed documentation regarding their root causes, full business impact, incident response activities, and corrective actions has not yet been made available. Their recurrence potential and the effectiveness of any remediation measures will require further assessment.

### 6. Business Drivers for Cybersecurity Risk Management

NovaCart's dependence on digital business services, international customer relationships, and external technology providers creates a need for a structured approach to cybersecurity risk management.

The organisation must be able to identify potential events that could affect its business operations, understand the weaknesses that may increase its exposure, and evaluate the consequences of cybersecurity incidents. This includes risks associated with customer information, employee identities, cloud infrastructure, business applications, payment integrations, and third-party service providers.

Effective risk management is important for protecting business-critical information, maintaining the availability of customer-facing services, reducing the likelihood and impact of security incidents, and supporting informed management decisions.

As the organisation operates across multiple international markets, its information handling and security obligations may also differ depending on the jurisdictions, customers, and third-party relationships involved. Relevant contractual, privacy, and regulatory requirements will need to be identified and considered within the assessment.

A structured cybersecurity risk register will provide a central mechanism for documenting identified risks, evaluating their significance, assigning accountability, and monitoring agreed treatment activities.

### 7. Requirement for a Cybersecurity GRC Risk Assessment

The Information Security Department has identified the need for a structured cybersecurity risk assessment to establish a documented understanding of NovaCart's information security risk exposure.

The assessment will examine the organisation's business environment, technology dependencies, and information assets to identify potential cybersecurity threats and vulnerabilities. It will evaluate identified risks using a consistent methodology, consider the effectiveness of existing security controls, and document the potential consequences of security incidents.

The GRC assessment will also identify areas where additional information or supporting evidence is required before reliable risk decisions can be made. This will enable the assessment team to distinguish confirmed information from assumptions and outstanding questions.

The findings will be consolidated into a cybersecurity risk register that records risk descriptions, affected assets, threats, vulnerabilities, existing controls, inherent and residual risk ratings, risk ownership, and recommended treatment actions.

The resulting risk treatment plan will provide management with prioritised recommendations to address identified risks and support ongoing risk monitoring and governance.

### 8. Intended Business Outcomes

The assessment is intended to provide NovaCart's management with a structured view of its cybersecurity risk exposure and the areas requiring improvement.

The expected outcomes include improved visibility of critical information assets, documented understanding of cybersecurity threats and vulnerabilities, consistent evaluation of risk levels, and better understanding of existing security control effectiveness.

The assessment will also support the assignment of risk ownership, prioritisation of remediation activities, and monitoring of residual risks. Relevant outcomes from the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0 will be used as a reference to organise and communicate cybersecurity risk management activities.

Ultimately, the project aims to establish a practical and traceable cybersecurity risk management approach that supports business objectives while helping the organisation make informed decisions about its information security priorities.
