# NovaCart Retail Ltd. – Cybersecurity GRC Risk Register

**An End-to-End Cybersecurity Risk Assessment and Risk Treatment Portfolio Project**

---

## 1. Project Overview

This project demonstrates the application of Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) principles through the development of a structured cybersecurity risk register and risk treatment plan for a fictional, medium-sized global e-commerce organisation, NovaCart Retail Ltd.

The project follows a practical risk assessment approach, beginning with an understanding of the organisation's business environment and identification of its information assets, followed by threat and vulnerability identification, risk analysis, evidence evaluation, and risk treatment planning.

The objective is to demonstrate how a GRC analyst identifies, evaluates, documents, prioritises, and communicates cybersecurity risks to support informed business decision-making.

The assessment uses the **NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0** as a reference for organising cybersecurity risk management activities.

## 2. Business Scenario

NovaCart Retail Ltd. is a fictional e-commerce organisation headquartered in London, United Kingdom, with customers across multiple international markets.

The organisation employs approximately 650 people and generates an estimated annual revenue of USD 45 million. Its primary business operations involve selling consumer electronics, home appliances, and lifestyle products through its e-commerce website and mobile application.

Its technology environment includes:

- AWS-hosted cloud infrastructure.
- E-commerce website and mobile application.
- Customer relationship management (CRM) platform.
- Enterprise resource planning (ERP) platform.
- Customer information repositories.
- External payment service integrations.
- Third-party logistics and marketing providers.
- Employee accounts and corporate devices.

The organisation processes customer information, employee records, financial information, transaction references, and other business-sensitive data.

Given its reliance on interconnected digital systems and external service providers, NovaCart faces potential cybersecurity risks involving unauthorised access, phishing, ransomware, data exposure, service disruption, and third-party security incidents.

All company details and scenario information are fictional and created exclusively for this educational portfolio project.

## 3. Project Objectives

The primary objectives of this project are to:

1. Understand the organisation's business environment and technology dependencies.
2. Identify and classify critical information assets.
3. Identify relevant cybersecurity threats and vulnerabilities.
4. Establish a consistent risk assessment methodology.
5. Develop an initial cybersecurity risk register.
6. Identify information gaps and evaluate supporting evidence.
7. Assess existing security controls and their effectiveness.
8. Reassess risks using additional evidence.
9. Develop a risk treatment plan with proposed actions and ownership.
10. Map relevant cybersecurity risk management activities to NIST CSF 2.0.
11. Prepare a consolidated risk assessment report for management.

## 4. Project Methodology

The project follows a structured risk assessment lifecycle.

### Phase 1 – Business Context and Assessment Scope

Review the organisation's business operations, technology environment, information assets, assessment objectives, and scope boundaries.

### Phase 2 – Asset and Threat Identification

Identify important business and information assets, determine their criticality, and analyse potential threats and vulnerabilities affecting them.

### Phase 3 – Initial Risk Assessment

Develop an initial risk register using the information available from the business scenario. Evaluate inherent risks using the defined likelihood and impact criteria.

### Phase 4 – Evidence and Control Assessment

Identify information gaps, establish evidence requirements, and review additional simulated evidence to evaluate existing security controls.

### Phase 5 – Updated Risk Assessment

Reassess identified risks using the additional evidence. Document changes in risk ratings and explain the reasons behind each material change.

### Phase 6 – Risk Treatment Planning

Recommend appropriate risk treatment strategies, define remediation activities, identify proposed risk owners, and establish target completion dates.

### Phase 7 – NIST CSF 2.0 Mapping and Final Reporting

Map relevant risks and treatment activities to NIST CSF 2.0 outcomes and prepare a consolidated management-level report.

## 5. Risk Assessment Methodology

The project uses a qualitative 5 × 5 risk assessment matrix.

**Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact**

Likelihood and impact are assessed on a scale from 1 to 5.

| Risk Score | Risk Level |
|---|---|
| 1–4 | Low |
| 5–9 | Medium |
| 10–16 | High |
| 17–25 | Critical |

The assessment distinguishes between:

- **Inherent Risk:** Risk exposure before considering existing security controls.
- **Residual Risk:** Risk remaining after considering the effectiveness of existing controls.

Risk treatment recommendations consider four approaches:

- Risk Mitigation
- Risk Avoidance
- Risk Transfer
- Risk Acceptance

Risk ratings and treatment decisions will be supported by documented analysis and available evidence. The scoring methodology is defined for this portfolio exercise and is not presented as a prescribed NIST scoring model.

## 7. Framework and References

These references provide supporting guidance for risk identification, analysis, control assessment, and cybersecurity risk management.
